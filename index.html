Differential Geometry of Plane Curves

A Comprehensive Study of Curves in the Plane

1. Introduction to Plane Curves

Definition

A plane curve is a continuous mapping from an interval $I \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ to the plane $\mathbb{R}^2$. We typically represent a plane curve parametrically as:

$$\pp(t) = (x(t), y(t))$$

where $t \in I$ is the parameter and $x(t)$, $y(t)$ are continuous functions.

Basic Properties

Plane curves can be characterized by several fundamental properties:

Smoothness

A curve is smooth if the functions $x(t)$ and $y(t)$ are differentiable and the derivative $\pp'(t) = (x'(t), y'(t))$ is never zero (i.e., $\pp'(t) \neq \mathbf{0}$ for all $t$).

Regularity

A curve is regular if it is smooth and the tangent vector $\pp'(t)$ has non-zero magnitude for all $t$ in the domain.

Why is regularity important?

  • Ensures well-defined tangent directions at every point
  • Enables computation of geometric quantities like curvature
  • Prevents singularities (cusps, stops) that would break our analysis
  • Essential for defining unit tangent vectors and normal vectors
  • Required for meaningful arc length parameterization

Examples of Plane Curves

Circle

A circle of radius $R$ centered at the origin:

$$\pp(t) = (R\cos t, R\sin t), \quad t \in [0, 2\pi]$$

Properties:

  • Tangent vector: $\pp'(t) = (-R\sin t, R\cos t)$
  • Speed: $|\pp'(t)| = R$ (constant)
  • This is a regular curve

Ellipse

An ellipse with semi-axes $a$ and $b$:

$$\pp(t) = (a\cos t, b\sin t), \quad t \in [0, 2\pi]$$

Properties:

  • Tangent vector: $\pp'(t) = (-a\sin t, b\cos t)$
  • Speed: $|\pp'(t)| = \sqrt{a^2\sin^2 t + b^2\cos^2 t}$
  • When $a = b$, this reduces to a circle

Parabola

A parabola opening upward:

$$\pp(t) = (t, t^2), \quad t \in \mathbb{R}$$

Properties:

  • Tangent vector: $\pp'(t) = (1, 2t)$
  • Speed: $|\pp'(t)| = \sqrt{1 + 4t^2}$
  • This is a regular curve for all $t$

Non-Regular Curve Example

A semicubical parabola (also called a cusp curve):

$$\pp(t) = (t^2, t^3), \quad t \in \mathbb{R}$$

Properties:

  • Tangent vector: $\pp'(t) = (2t, 3t^2)$
  • At $t = 0$: $\pp'(0) = (0, 0)$ - not regular!
  • The curve has a cusp at the origin
  • This is smooth but not regular at $t = 0$

Why this matters: At the cusp, we cannot define a tangent direction, unit tangent vector, or compute curvature. This demonstrates why the regularity condition is essential for differential geometric analysis.

Cycloid

A cycloid is the curve traced by a point on the rim of a rolling circle:

$$\pp(t) = R(t - \sin t, 1 - \cos t)$$

Properties:

  • Tangent vector: $\pp'(t) = R(1 - \cos t, \sin t)$
  • At $t = 2\pi n$: $\pp'(t) = (0, 0)$ - cusps!
  • The curve has periodic cusps every $2\pi$
  • This is smooth but not regular at cusp points

Applications: The cycloid appears in gear design, pendulum clocks (tautochrone property), and optimal path problems in physics.

Key Formulas

For a parametric curve $\pp(t) = (x(t), y(t))$:

  • Tangent vector: $\pp'(t) = (x'(t), y'(t))$
  • Speed: $|\pp'(t)| = \sqrt{(x'(t))^2 + (y'(t))^2}$
  • Unit tangent vector: $\TT(t) = \frac{\pp'(t)}{|\pp'(t)|}$
